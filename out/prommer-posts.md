## Founders evaluating a counterparty

I've run engineering under real numbers: 500+ engineers on a $9B+ e-commerce operation across 50 markets at Adidas, and I grew Huge's technology practice from 50 to 500+ engineers across 14 offices, delivering for Apple, Google, McDonald's, Toyota, and Nike. What broke, repeatedly, was AI governance without ownership. As I told CIO.inc, most enterprises need named technical and business sponsors co-owning outcomes, embedded AI squads instead of a central Center of Excellence, and stage-gated outcome funding — not blanket mandates. Mandates fail when people won't let their best work get footnoted as machine-made. If you're evaluating me as a counterparty: ask what I'd stage-gate first in your org, and I'll tell you within a week.

## Operators running AI-native teams

Copy this one practice: stop funding AI work in one lump sum. Stage-gate outcome funding, tied to named sponsors — one technical, one business — who co-own the result, not just the rollout. I described this as one of three fixes for the accountability gap in CIO.com's State of the CIO 2026 coverage, alongside replacing a central Center of Excellence with embedded AI squads. It forces a decision point before the next dollar moves, instead of after the budget's spent. This week: pick one live AI initiative, name its two co-owners, and set the first gate before Friday.
